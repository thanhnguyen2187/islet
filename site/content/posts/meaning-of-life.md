Let's say upfront that I have an answer to the question that might be a tad
better than "42" from The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy. I'm using the words

- "meaning" as an adjacent to "purpose", and this "purpose" is human-centered
- "life" is limited to humanity's, or an average person's lifespan

As if we expand the scope of "purpose" to the Universe itself, and "life" to all
"living" things on this Earth, I'd lean a lot on the side of "no meaning", since
it's easily seen that at that scale, what humanity do as a whole and as
individuals doesn't really affect the universe. I believe we are not (yet) able
to make such change.

Beware and be wary that I'm not that well-versed in philosophy (I might know a
thing or two in Eastern philosophy and Taoism, but next to none in the Western
side) nor religion (my experience with Catholic/Orthodox/Christianity is limited
from being preached on). My term usage might not be accurate either, and I
assume we have "common definitions" on some words. I'll elaborate when I feel
that there are more nuances.

Getting back to the question and our definition, my answer would start with a
more common phrase, "it depends". The natural follow up is "depends on what?".
The final piece to the puzzle is "belief". In a sense, "faith" would be a more
appropriate word, it sounds more religious than I wanted, while I preferred to
keep it neutral.

"Belief" is the central of my pet theory:

- Deep down, you have some thing or many things that you trust, whether you are
  aware of their existence or not
- Using beliefs, as something akin to mathematical axioms, might help you
  clearly explain and analyze a lot of your behaviors (and maybe others' as
  well)

Making your belief clear is an important prestep to define and choose what's the
"meaning"/"puporse" you'd want your life to revolve around. To choose might make
you want to ask about "freewill", or whether we have our own choices despite the
Universe's arrangement. I'd rather not get into the details. My purpose is one
but to lay out my thoughts, and have that evoke your thoughts. I won't nor can't
outright telling you what should you do nor think, but I hope what I'm about to
write can be interesting enough and help you on deciding what your belief is and
the meaning you're going to decide on. 

To determine one's belief, regarding to their meaning of life, I find it useful
to use a theism/religious lense first. In other words, it's to categorize the
belief around "God" (loosely speaking, let's treat God as an entity that's
"above" humanity in the sense that he or she can do what we can't physically):

- We cannot prove nor disprove the entity's existence: agnosticism
- There's no such entity: atheism
- There's one or many such entities: theism (monotheism or polytheism)

My current belief is in the agnosticism camp, and to narrow it down further,
it's "apathetic agnosticism", or

> I don't know whether any deity exists or not, and I don't care if any deity
> exists or not [^wikipedia-agnosticism]

Relating to the society/others, I'm aware that I'm both significant to my
friends and family, and insignificant to the society as a whole (without me the
world still move on). I can either choose my meaning to be around my friends and
family, or decide that I'm not that important, and "life has no meaning" nor
serves any higher purpose. For the first case, seems like we've solved
something. However, for the second case, one may ask:

- "If there's no meaning, why don't you just... die?"

And I'd go with the opposite question:

- "If there's no meaning, why don't you just... live?"

It's an interesting status quo that couldn't be resolved directly. Maybe one way
to get out of it is to treat the finding itself as the answer. In other words,
the meaning of life is to search for the meaning. I'm assuming that only when
we're in a living state that we can still think, and do the search. Getting to
an other case, we still get stuck at deciding between living and dying, I'd
assume that we need to live to get stuck.

In any case, we need be alive.

Atheism might be easier to get through: since we don't believe that there's a
God, nor a higher deity, we instead select another "object", such as science
(For simplicity's sake, let's say we are understanding science as a huge set of
different disciplines, and categorize them to 2 groups, social science which is
human-related and natural science, which is not). I'm sure that people who are
more well-versed than me in social science can have their own derivation, but
I'd just use a simple finding from biology: the meaning of live is to survive,
and reproduce. In society's point of view, it's good as well: a living
individual that blends well enough to reproduce would help itself and bring
values to the people around.

In other words, we need to be alive, and have sex.
